# Optimization Algorithm for Whales and Its Application in Wireless Network Resource Allocation [with MATLAB Code]

Abstract: Resource allocation plays a crucial role in enhancing the performance of wireless and communication networks. However, the optimization problem of resource allocation is often described as a mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problem, which is essentially non-convex and NP-hard. Typically, due to the main drawbacks of traditional methods such as exponential computational complexity for global optimization, lack of performance optimality guarantees for heuristic approaches, and training time for machine learning-based methods along with generation of standard datasets, solving such problems is challenging and requires specific methods. The whale optimization algorithm, as an effective method for solving various optimization problems, has gained significant attention in the academic community in recent years. As an alternative to existing methods, this paper primarily aims to investigate the applicability of the Wireless Analysis Method (WAM) in addressing wireless network resource allocation issues. Firstly, we introduce the basic background and binary version of WOA, and introduce a penalty method to handle optimization constraints. Then, we present three examples of WOA in wireless network resource allocation, including power allocation in wireless interference networks where energy and spectral efficiency trade-offs are considered, maximizing security throughput power allocation, and mobile edge computing offloading. Finally, we propose employing WOA to solve various resource allocation issues that may arise in 5G wireless networks and beyond.
Introduction A. Motivation and Related Work
Resource optimization in wireless networks, such as power allocation for spectrum and energy efficiency, subcarrier allocation in Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access (OFDMA) and Multi-Carrier Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA), time allocation in Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA), computation offloading in Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC), remote radio head selection in Cloud Radio Access Networks (C-RAN), and cluster head selection in Wireless Sensor Networks, is typically described as a Mixed Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) problem [1]. These problems are difficult to solve and often may be NP-hard. Numerous optimization and learning techniques have been proposed to obtain solutions for MINLP problems, some of which include:
1) Global Optimization: Some algorithms, such as branch and bound (BNB) and dynamic programming, can be used to obtain the global optimal solution for any MINLP problem. However, the exponential worst-case complexity limits their implementation and application in wireless networks, as channels are often highly dynamic, and a vast number of Internet of Things (IoT) devices with cellular connections will be available in the coming years.
2) Heuristic Methods: To reduce the exponential computational complexity of global optimization methods, heuristic algorithms have been widely used in literature. An example of heuristic algorithm is relaxing integer variables to continuous ones and using quadratic constraints to approximate binary constraints. For instance, subcarrier allocation for device-to-device (D2D) pairs d can be represented by Xcd if the D2D pair is allowed to reuse the subcarriers of cellular user (CU) c, where xcd=1, otherwise xcd=0[3]. By relaxing, Xcd can be represented as ˆxcd=[0,1], which can be interpreted as the fraction of time D2D pair d reuses the subcarriers from CUc. However, one major drawback of low-complexity heuristic algorithms is that they cannot guarantee convergence to an optimal solution, thus resulting in performance gaps compared to the optimal solution.
3) Game Theory Approaches: Game theory provides a mathematical tool for studying interactions between independent rational participants. Recently, game theory has become an important tool for solving many problems in wireless and communication networks. For example, utilizing joint matching theory and coalition games to solve resource allocation problems with D2D communication in heterogeneous C-RAN[4] and UAV communication transmission theory[5]. Although game theory is useful and widely applied, it is based on rational reactions and relies on mathematical models that may affect the strategies and outcomes of game participants, making it unsuitable in some cases.
4) Machine Learning (ML) based methods: ML has recently been integrated into wireless and communication networks as a disruptive technology that can balance performance and computational complexity [6], [7]. However, there are certain tasks that may fail to utilize ML in wireless networks. For instance, generating standard training datasets for problems is not an easy task, and sometimes it is unavailable. Additionally, training neural networks with large datasets and a large number of parameters is a time-consuming process.
To provide a viable alternative to address complex resource allocation issues in wireless communication, we aim to leverage metaheuristic algorithms. Inspired by these algorithms, we apply them to numerous large-scale and real-world engineering optimization problems, such as electrical engineering [8], civil engineering [9], mechanical engineering [10], and industrial engineering [11]. Recently, Mirjalili et al. proposed a new metaheuristic algorithm, the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) [12], which mimics the hunting behavior of humpback whales. Humpback whales are often considered predators, with their primary prey being krill and small fish. One of the most popular feeding methods for humpback whales is bubble netting, a unique behavior only observed on them. In bubble netting, humpback whales swim in circles with reduced speed and blow bubbles below prey, forcing them to fly towards the surface [12]. Literature [12] indicates that WOA outperforms state-of-the-art metaheuristic algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Gravitational Search Algorithm (GSA), Genetic Algorithm (GA), and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) in nearly all test scenarios. In wireless network environments, mobile users can be viewed as humpback whales, while the optimal solution to the optimization variables can be seen as prey. By emulating the hunting behavior of humpback whales, various optimization problems in wireless networks can be solved using the highly competitive WOA. Recent versions of WOA have been proposed, including its binary version [13]-[15], a balance between exploration and exploitation [16], Chaotic WOA [17], and WOA for optimizing neural networks [18].
B. Our Contributions
As mentioned above, WOA has significant applications across various disciplines; however, there is no work found that provides an application or suitability of WOA in wireless and communication networks. The main purpose of this paper is to study the WOA algorithm, provide a concise tutorial on the WOA algorithm, and discuss its potential applications in various resource allocation optimization problems. The main contributions can be summarized as follows:
We first provide a brief overview of WOA, including its mathematical model and optimization algorithm. Since the original WOA is only applicable to continuous unconstrained optimization problems, we propose a binary version of WOA (BWOA) and introduce penalty methods to handle optimization constraints. Combining the original WOA with BWOA and penalty methods can solve a wide range of optimization problems and achieve higher quality solutions.
To illustrate the applicability of WOA, we investigated three resource allocation problems in wireless networks: maximizing security throughput, balancing energy and spectrum efficiency, and mobility-edge computing offloading. The WOA algorithm was then applied to solve these problems. Simulation results show that the algorithm converges quickly and performs comparably to existing algorithms.
We have outlined some potential applications of WOA in the trajectory optimization for UAVs, interference management in ultra-dense networks (UDN), user association and scheduling, mode selection, and support for multicarrier NOMA in MEC systems. Experimental results indicate that the WOA algorithm is a promising solution for solving the problem of wireless network resource allocation optimization.
